3 · Single-agent lookups vs. multi-agent orchestration

Some operations were simple record retrievals, not ambiguous cross-functional decisions.

  • Ticket 102 lease lookup: one MCP read of leases plus desk.date_today is enough to compare the lease’s next_due. A single agent with those MCP tools could answer the date question without a Boss → Accounting handoff.
  • Ticket 101 / 103 stock count: a direct inventory lookup by SKU and size answers available units and shortfall. A specialist handoff adds little for that isolated read, although ticket 101’s vendor/invoice hold and ticket 103’s price authority require more context.
  • Cash and unpaid bills: one read-only financial snapshot of cash_accounts, invoices, and payments is enough to report balances and open invoices. Delegating the lookup and then repeating broad financial context adds latency and input tokens.

The audit shows several MCP calls per successful run and specialist model invocations in addition to the Boss. Every handoff adds a model request, prompt/tool schemas, returned findings, and audit events. Multi-agent work is more useful when it combines distinct judgments—such as Inventory identifying the ticket 101 vendor hold while Accounting assesses invoice #501—than when it simply wraps a single-table read.

4 · Three adjacent problems this team could help solve

1. Inventory restock recommendations

Inventory could compare quantities in inventory with agreed reorder thresholds, check vendor lead times in vendors, and review open invoices before recommending a restock. Important limitation: the current inventory schema has no reorder-threshold field, so a threshold must first be added or supplied as an approved policy; agents must not invent one.

2. Overdue lease follow-up and Accounting flags

Facilities/Accounting could compare leases.next_due with desk.date_today, identify upcoming or past-due leases, and flag them for review. The requested field name leases.due_date does not exist, and neither penalty amounts nor a penalty formula are in the current schema; the team can flag a date, but cannot calculate a valid penalty without approved rules and data.

3. Cash-aware multi-invoice payment planning

Accounting can inspect open rows in invoices alongside cash_accounts and prioritize a proposed set that stays within recorded cash. It can present the plan for human review. The current report model holds one payment draft at a time and the approval route executes one reference per request, so true atomic batch approval/execution would require extending those interfaces; every invoice still needs explicit human authorization.

5 · Three problems the current team cannot solve by itself

1. Physical warehouse or shop-space reorganization

The inventory table has stock quantities and location labels, not measured floor plans, dimensions, safety clearances, or live shelf occupancy. This needs a Logistics Agent plus spatial/layout tools (for example, a maintained floor-plan model and verified measurements) and human onsite validation.

2. Live payment processing or bank wires

The current app records approved local outflows in SQLite payments and adjusts cash_accounts; it has no bank rail, settlement confirmation, or external payment gateway. This needs a separately authorized Secure Payment Gateway Agent, authenticated banking API integration, idempotency/reconciliation controls, and a human approval workflow. The existing local approval route is not a bank transfer.

3. Dynamic customer pricing and negotiation

The current pricing data provides unit cost and list price, but no demand curves, competitor prices, customer history, or discount authority—seen directly in ticket 103. This needs a Sales Strategy Agent, vetted market/competitor and demand-analysis tools, an approved pricing policy, and human oversight before any customer quote.
